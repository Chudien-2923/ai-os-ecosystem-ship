# Customer journey source fixture

- Source type: workshop note
- Received: 2026-10-27

A customer journey describes the sequence of situations in which a person becomes aware of a problem, considers ways to solve it, chooses an option, experiences delivery, and decides whether to continue or recommend it. The journey is not identical to a marketing funnel: a funnel is usually the organization's stage model, while the journey is the customer's lived progression and can move backward or skip stages.

For a sample online store, useful evidence includes the triggering job-to-be-done, questions asked before purchase, objections, time-to-first-value after purchase, completion friction, and reasons customers continue or stop using the product. Teams should not assume every touchpoint caused the purchase merely because it appeared earlier in the path.

A practical exercise is to interview five recent buyers and five qualified non-buyers, map their trigger, alternatives, anxieties, decision event, first-value moment, and drop-off point, then compare those observations with analytics rather than replacing interviews with click-path data.
